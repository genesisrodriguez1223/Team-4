# Team Scope Agreement


Commit, in writing, to what your team will actually have working by the end of the semester.

This is the assignment where optimism meets the calendar. You have roughly two sprints. Whatever you agree to here is what the sprint 2 and final demonstrations will be judged against — not against your ambitions in September, and not against a moving target you revise every time something slips.

What to submit
The committed scope — the specific functionality that will be demonstrably working at session 26.
Explicitly deferred functionality — what you wanted and are consciously not doing.
The traceability line from each committed item back to a requirement in T2.
Named risks to delivery, with what you would cut first if a sprint goes badly.
Underscope deliberately
A team that commits to four features and demonstrates four working features scores better than a team that commits to nine and demonstrates six half-working ones. This is not a trick — it is how the rubric is built, and it is also how delivery actually works. Deciding what to cut before you are under pressure is the professional skill; deciding it at 2 a.m. the night before the demo is not.

Rubric , Criterion , Pts

Committed scope is specific enough to be demonstrated and judged - 6

Scope is realistic for two sprints with this team - 5

Deferred functionality is named rather than left ambiguous - 3

Every committed item traces to a T2 requirement - 3

Delivery risks are named with a stated cut order - 3
